If you've got a grill brush hanging in your garage or stuffed in a drawer near the patio, there's a real chance it's been recalled. Not hypothetically recalled, not "under review", actually pulled from the market by the CPSC because people swallowed metal bristles and ended up in the hospital. Back-to-back recalls in February and March 2026 have now flagged more than 13 million wire-bristle grill brushes sold under the Weber and Nexgrill names. Grilling season is here. This is the moment to go check.

## What Got Recalled and Whether You Have One

Weber's recall came first. On February 26, 2026, the CPSC announced Weber was pulling over 3.2 million metal wire-bristle grill brushes due to a design flaw that allows individual bristles to detach during normal use. The problem isn't just that bristles fall off. It's that they're thin enough to land on your grate, transfer to food, and get swallowed without anyone noticing. Weber's brushes covered by this recall were sold at Lowe's, Home Depot, Ace Hardware, Target, and Amazon between 2011 and 2026, mostly in the $10–$17 range.

Then Nexgrill followed exactly a month later. On March 26, 2026, the CPSC announced Nexgrill was recalling over 10.2 million wire-bristle brushes sold exclusively through Home Depot and homedepot.com from 2015 through 2026, priced between $5 and $15. Together, that's more than 13 million brushes across two major brands, sold through virtually every major retailer most homeowners shop.

The CPSC documented at least 68 reports from Nexgrill users and 38 from Weber users of bristles detaching. Nine people across both brands swallowed bristles and needed medical treatment. That might sound like a small number relative to 13 million brushes, but those are just the reported cases. Plenty of people don't connect a sudden pain or a trip to urgent care to a grill brush they used three days ago.

To check if your specific brush is included, go directly to cpsc.gov and search the recall database. The CPSC listings for both the Weber and Nexgrill recalls include model numbers, photos, and instructions for getting a refund.

## Why This Hazard Is Harder to Catch Than It Sounds

You might be wondering: wouldn't you notice a wire bristle in your food? Usually, no. These bristles are thin, flexible, and can embed in a burger or a chicken thigh in a way that's invisible to the eye and easy to miss when chewing. Once swallowed, a wire bristle can perforate the esophagus, stomach lining, or intestines. That's not a minor issue you sleep off. It can require surgery.

CPSC Chair Peter Feldman made the stakes clear when the recalls were announced: don't wait for grilling season to deal with this. Stop using wire-bristle brushes now and switch to a non-wire alternative. That guidance from the CPSC isn't boilerplate. It's a direct response to the fact that the injury risk isn't obvious at the point of use. There's no moment where the brush looks broken or the food looks contaminated. Everything seems fine until it isn't.

This is also why the recalls cover such long date ranges. A brush you bought in 2014 and have used carefully for a decade could shed a bristle today. Age and heavy use tend to make the problem worse, not better.

## What to Use Instead

Here's what I tell people when they ask what to buy: the alternatives work just as well, and some of them work better. The wire brush became a default tool because it was cheap and available everywhere, not because it was the best option for grill cleaning.

The most direct swap is a coiled stainless steel scraper. These use a tightly wound coil design instead of loose bristles, and they're effective on both gas and charcoal grates. There's no loose material to shed, and they hold up well over a season. Brands like Kona and Grill Art make well-reviewed versions in the $15–$25 range.

Nylon-bristle brushes are another option, recommended by safety experts and the CDC as a wire-free alternative. They work best when the grate has cooled slightly, since nylon can melt on a screaming hot grate. Worth knowing before you buy.

Pumice grill stones are underrated. They grind off carbon buildup without any bristle risk at all, and they're inexpensive. You hold one with the stone holder (or a stick, if you're improvising) and scrub the grate directly.

And then there's the dead-simple approach that's been around forever: crumple up a piece of aluminum foil into a ball, grab it with long tongs, and scrub. It works. So does cutting an onion in half and rubbing the cut side across a hot grate. The natural oils loosen residue, and you're not introducing anything to the grate you wouldn't want in your food.

## What To Do Right Now If You Own One of These Brushes

Don't just put the brush in a cabinet. That creates the same risk the next time someone reaches for it. Take it out of rotation entirely.

For the Weber recall, the CPSC is directing consumers to stop use immediately and contact Weber for a full refund. For the Nexgrill recall, same instruction: stop use and contact Nexgrill or visit the CPSC recall page for refund details. You don't need a receipt for most recall refunds. The manufacturer and retailer are typically required to make the process straightforward. If you bought yours at Home Depot, that's worth a direct call to their customer service line as well.

If you've already experienced a bristle detaching, or if someone in your household had an unexplained throat or stomach injury after a cookout, the AWKO Law recall resource page has information on consumer rights and reporting options beyond just the refund process.

The timing here matters. Both recalls landed in the two months before grilling season peaks, which means millions of people pulled these brushes out of winter storage and may be using them right now without knowing they're recalled. The CPSC published both recall pages with clear photos and model details, and checking takes about two minutes.

Pick up a coiled scraper or a pumice stone before your next cookout. It's a small change that eliminates a real risk, and honestly, you'll probably find the replacement works just as well as what you had.
