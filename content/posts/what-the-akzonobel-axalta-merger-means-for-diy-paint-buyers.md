The two biggest names in professional and DIY coatings are about to become one. On November 6, 2026, the European Commission conditionally approved the all-stock merger between AkzoNobel and Axalta, clearing the biggest obstacle in a deal [that has](/how-to-fix-a-tile-that-has-come-loose/) been building all year. The UK's Competition and Markets Authority followed five days later with a Phase 1 decision that added another layer of scrutiny, but both companies said on November 11 that they're still confident the deal closes by early 2027. If you buy Dulux at the hardware store, use Hammerite on garden metalwork, or take your car to a body shop that sprays Cromax, this consolidation affects you more than you might think.

The combined company would be the world's largest coatings operation, with AkzoNobel shareholders holding 55% and Axalta shareholders holding 45%. Current AkzoNobel CEO Greg Poux-Guillaume will run the merged entity. And the brand lineup is striking in how much of the market it covers: AkzoNobel brings Dulux, Flexa, and Hammerite on the consumer side, while Axalta contributes Cromax, Standox, and Spies Hecker in professional refinish. This isn't a niche deal. It's a structural reshaping of the shelves you shop from and the products your contractor orders.

What makes this deal even more telling is how it got here. In April 2026, Nippon Paint and Sherwin-Williams made a competing cash bid for AkzoNobel. AkzoNobel rejected it on May 1, 2026, and pivoted toward Axalta instead. The fact that two of the world's largest coatings companies were circling AkzoNobel at the same time tells you something about where this industry is heading: toward a handful of very large players.

## What the Divestiture Condition Actually Means

The EC didn't approve this deal without strings. AkzoNobel is required to sell off its European vehicle refinish business as a condition of approval, and the UK CMA's Phase 1 decision means additional review is ongoing. According to the SEC Form 425 filing from November 11, both companies say they intend to apply the same divestiture remedy in the UK that satisfied the European Commission.

That divestiture matters to body shop owners and refinish suppliers, but for the average DIY buyer it's a bit of a sideshow. The vehicle refinish segment, which includes brands like Standox and Spies Hecker, runs through professional collision repair channels, not your local paint department. What it does signal, though, is that regulators are watching product overlap carefully. That scrutiny is less intense on the consumer retail side, which means Dulux and Flexa face fewer mandated protections.

## How Paint Consolidation Historically Hits Retail Shelves

Mergers at this scale don't usually cause prices to jump the week the deal closes. The more typical pattern plays out over 18 to 36 months, as the combined company rationalizes its product lines, cuts redundant SKUs, and renegotiates supplier contracts. What that means for you at the hardware store: fewer mid-tier options, more house brand or flagship brand positioning, and slower price competition because there's one less major manufacturer bidding for shelf space.

Here's a rough picture of how the brand landscape shifts before and after:

| Brand | Segment | Pre-Merger Owner | Post-Merger Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dulux | DIY / retail decorative | AkzoNobel | Combined entity |
| Flexa | DIY / retail decorative | AkzoNobel | Combined entity |
| Hammerite | DIY metalwork coatings | AkzoNobel | Combined entity |
| Cromax | Professional refinish | Axalta | Combined entity (or divested in EU) |
| Standox | Professional refinish | Axalta | Combined entity (or divested in EU) |
| Spies Hecker | Professional refinish | Axalta | Combined entity (or divested in EU) |

That's a lot of brand names still on the shelf, but fewer decision-makers setting the price behind each of them. Sherwin-Williams, PPG, and BASF Coatings still compete at the professional level, but in DIY retail, Dulux in particular dominates European markets so thoroughly that the competitive check on pricing is already limited.

## What DIY Buyers Should Watch For

If you're a homeowner who does your own painting, the near-term practical concern isn't price gouging. It's availability and product continuity. When large paint companies merge, reformulations happen. A product you've been using for years, matched in a specific finish or tint base, can get quietly discontinued or reformulated under a consolidated product line. That's not speculation; it's what happened across segments after several earlier Sherwin-Williams acquisitions and after PPG absorbed several regional brands.

A few things worth doing now: if you have a paint match you rely on, buy an extra gallon or two before the merger fully closes and document the exact formula code on your receipt. Tint formulas are stored by retailers, but those databases can get updated when manufacturers push new base formulas. Getting your formula on paper costs nothing.

Also pay attention to Hammerite specifically. It's a niche but genuinely useful product for anyone painting over rust on garden gates, radiators, or metal fixtures. It doesn't have a lot of direct retail competition in its category, and brands in that position sometimes see price increases or reduced can sizes after consolidation, since there's less market pressure to hold the line.

## For Homeowners Hiring Contractors

If you're hiring a painter or a body shop rather than doing the work yourself, the main question is whether your contractor will have access to the same products at the same price point a year from now. Professional painting contractors who spec Dulux for interior work are largely insulated from short-term disruption. The supply chain for decorative trade products is robust enough that a merger of equals, executed over 12-plus months, rarely causes shortages.

The vehicle refinish side is more complicated. Body shops in Europe that use Standox or Spies Hecker should be watching the divestiture process closely. Those brands may change hands, change distribution, or change pricing structures depending on who buys AkzoNobel's refinish business. If you're managing a fleet or have an ongoing body shop relationship, ask your contact what they're hearing from their rep. They'll know before it hits the news.

The deal isn't closed yet, and the UK CMA could still require additional remedies. But the direction of travel is clear. By early 2027, the global coatings market will likely have one fewer major independent player, and the brands on your shelf will answer to a larger consolidated balance sheet. That's not necessarily bad for quality, but it does change the competitive pressure that has historically kept prices honest and product lines varied. Watch your formulas, track your receipts, and stay aware of what's on the shelf today.
