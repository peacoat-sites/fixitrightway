The plank at the edge of my client's living room had been lifting for three months before she called me. She'd been stepping over it every single day, pressing it down with her toe each time she passed, telling herself she'd deal with it "soon." By the time I got there, the edge had developed a stress crack along the top layer and the locking tab underneath was half-broken. A repair that would've taken twenty minutes and zero dollars had turned into a $140 replacement job.

Don't be that person. Loose vinyl plank edges are fixable. But they have a window.

What most people don't realize is that there are actually three completely different reasons a vinyl plank edge comes loose, and the fix for each one is different. Treating them all the same is why people end up either wasting money on adhesive that doesn't hold, or pressing the floor back down only to watch it pop up again in two weeks.

## Figure Out Why It's Loose First

Kneel down and actually look at it. I mean get your face close to the floor. What you're trying to determine is whether the plank itself has separated from its neighbor (the click-lock joint failed), or whether the whole edge of the floor has shifted and is now riding up against the wall or a transition strip.

Press the loose plank down firmly and then release it. If it springs back up, that's usually a buckling problem caused by insufficient expansion gap, meaning the floor has nowhere to expand in heat and it's pushing upward at the edges. If it stays down but wobbles or rocks, you've probably got a broken locking tab or a failed adhesive bond. These feel completely different once you know what to look for.

There's a third scenario that catches people off guard: the subfloor underneath has developed a high spot, a low spot, or has gotten wet. I've seen brand-new floors start lifting at the edges within six months simply because someone's refrigerator had a slow drip that soaked the subfloor underneath the adjacent planks. Before you do anything else, check for moisture. Press on the plank. If you hear any squishing or feel any give that suggests softness underneath, stop and call a pro. That's a different problem entirely.

## The Expansion Gap Fix (Most Common Cause)

This is the one most homeowners don't expect. Luxury vinyl plank is a floating floor, meaning it's not attached to the subfloor. The whole installation relies on leaving a 1/4-inch gap around every wall and fixed object so the floor can expand and contract with temperature changes. If that gap got closed off (common after baseboards are reset too tight, or furniture legs push a plank over time), the floor has nowhere to go and it buckles upward at the weakest point: the edges.

Here's what you do. Remove the baseboard or quarter-round along the affected wall. A flat pry bar and a putty knife work fine. Take your time so you don't crack the baseboard. Once it's off, you'll likely see the plank is jammed right up against the drywall. Use a pull bar (a $12 tool from any hardware store) to gently tap the planks back toward the center of the room. You're working a few planks at a time, pulling them away from the wall.

Once you've re-established that 1/4-inch gap, reinstall the baseboard without nailing it tight to the floor. Nail it to the wall only. The baseboard just hides the gap. It should never pin the floor.

I've done this repair probably forty or fifty times over the years. The thing that always surprises people: how many planks you have to coax back before the lifted edge finally drops flat. Sometimes it's just three planks from the edge. Sometimes you're working your way back six feet. The floor is under tension, like a compressed spring.

## When the Click-Lock Joint Has Failed

If the expansion gap looks fine but the plank edge is still lifting, you're probably dealing with a separated or broken locking tab. This is where you need to make a decision.

If the tab is just disengaged (the plank slid slightly out of its neighbor), you may be able to re-engage it. Remove any transition strip covering that edge, lift the loose plank at a slight angle (typically 30-45 degrees), slide it back into position, and press down. You'll hear and feel it click. Sometimes it's really that simple.

If the tab is broken, you need to replace the plank. This is genuinely not difficult on an edge plank, since you don't have to disassemble half the room to get to it. Lift the damaged plank out, bring it to the store to match it, and click a new one in. The challenge is that vinyl plank floors fade and get discontinued constantly. If your floor is more than two years old, matching it perfectly is a real gamble.

Scenario: A homeowner in a three-year-old townhouse had one edge plank with a snapped locking tab near a [sliding door](/how-to-fix-a-sliding-door-that-wont-slide/). The original product had been discontinued. She ordered three planks in the closest match she could find from a different manufacturer ($31 for the planks, shipped). In direct lighting, you can tell. In normal room light, nobody notices.

## Glue-Down Edge Repairs

Some vinyl plank installations, especially older ones and commercial-grade products, are fully adhered to the subfloor. If you've got a glue-down floor and an edge is lifting, the adhesive has released. This happens from moisture, age, or subfloor movement.

The fix: get a flooring syringe (sold as "floor repair injectors" at most home centers, around $8-$14), fill it with flooring adhesive like Roberts 2057 or Henry 430, inject it under the lifted edge, press down, and weight it with books or a cinder block for 24 hours. The smell when you open flooring adhesive is genuinely strong. Do this with windows open. The adhesive is still working at about the four-hour mark but needs the full cure before you walk on it.

Do NOT do this with a floating floor. I want to be really clear about this because I see it constantly: if you've got a click-lock floating floor and you glue down a loose edge, you've just created a bigger problem. The floor can no longer move as a unit and you will get buckling elsewhere, usually within one seasonal cycle.

## What It Actually Costs

Here's a straightforward breakdown, current as of August 2026, based on what I'm seeing in the market.

| Repair Type | DIY Cost | Pro Labor Cost | Total w/ Pro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expansion gap reset (baseboard off + re-space) | $0-$15 (tools) | $75-$150/hr, ~1-2 hrs | $90-$315 |
| Re-engage disengaged click-lock tab | $0 | $75-$125 flat | $75-$125 |
| Replace single edge plank (tab broken) | $18-$55 (plank) | $125-$200 flat | $143-$255 |
| Glue-down adhesive re-bond | $12-$22 (adhesive + syringe) | $100-$175 flat | $112-$197 |
| Full subfloor moisture damage repair | Varies widely | $400-$1,800+ | $400-$1,800+ |

The wide range on the full subfloor repair reflects real variation: a small wet spot under two planks is very different from a 40-square-foot section of swollen OSB that needs replacement before the floor goes back down.
