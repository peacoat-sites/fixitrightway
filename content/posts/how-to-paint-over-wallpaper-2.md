Your walls are covered in old wallpaper, and peeling it off sounds like a nightmare. Maybe the wallpaper is original to the house, glued down in layers, or just too delicate to remove without damaging the drywall underneath. The good news? Learning **how to paint over wallpaper** is a real and practical solution that can save you hours of frustrating work. With the right preparation and materials, you can get a smooth, beautiful finish without ever picking up a wallpaper scraper.

## Is Painting Over Wallpaper a Good Idea?

Painting over wallpaper is not always the first choice of professional painters, but it is absolutely a valid option in many situations. It works especially well when the wallpaper is firmly bonded to the wall and removing it would cause serious damage to the drywall beneath.

However, this method does have some limitations. If the wallpaper is peeling, bubbling, or already coming away from the wall, painting over it will likely make those problems worse. Always assess the condition of your wallpaper before you begin.

For seniors who want to refresh a room without a major renovation, this approach is a great middle ground. It is less physically demanding than full removal and can be completed over a weekend.

## Tools and Materials You Will Need

Before you begin, gather everything you need so you are not making extra trips to the hardware store. Having the right tools on hand makes the job safer and more manageable.

  * Wallpaper seam repair adhesive or seam tape
  * Putty knife or wide drywall knife
  * Sandpaper (medium grit, around 120)
  * Oil-based primer or shellac-based primer
  * Interior latex paint in your chosen color
  * Paint roller and roller covers (3/8 inch nap)
  * Angled paintbrush (2.5 to 3 inches)
  * Painter’s tape
  * Drop cloths or plastic sheeting
  * Sponge and bucket of water
  * Joint compound or spackling paste
  * Safety goggles and dust mask

Using an oil-based or shellac primer is especially important here. Water-based primers can reactivate the wallpaper glue and cause bubbling or peeling, which is exactly what you want to avoid.

## Step 1: Inspect and Repair the Wallpaper

Start by walking around the room and carefully examining every inch of the wallpaper. Press gently on the surface to feel for loose spots, bubbles, or areas where the paper has separated from the wall.

Use your wallpaper seam adhesive to re-glue any lifting seams or peeling edges. Press them firmly into place and allow the adhesive to dry completely before moving on. This step is critical because paint will not hide raised seams ,  it will actually make them more visible.

For small tears or holes, apply a thin layer of joint compound with your putty knife. Let it dry fully, then sand it smooth. Wipe away any dust with a slightly damp sponge.

**Pro Tip:** Run your hand flat along every seam in the room, especially at corners and near baseboards. These are the spots most likely to lift when moisture from primer or paint is applied. A thorough repair job here will make the final result look dramatically smoother.

## Step 2: Clean the Walls Thoroughly

Dirty or greasy walls will prevent primer and paint from bonding properly. Mix a small amount of mild dish soap into a bucket of warm water and gently wipe down the entire surface using a soft sponge.

Pay extra attention to areas around light switches, near the stove or kitchen areas, and at hand height along hallways. These spots tend to collect oils and grime over the years.

Allow the walls to dry completely ,  ideally overnight. Applying primer to even slightly damp wallpaper can cause bubbling and lifting. Patience at this stage protects all the work you have done so far.

**⚠️ Safety:** When cleaning walls or working at heights on a ladder, always make sure someone else is home with you. Use a sturdy step stool or ladder rated for your weight, and never overreach. If you feel unsteady at any point, stop and reposition yourself before continuing. Falls are the leading cause of injury in home improvement projects for adults over 60.

## Step 3: Apply an Oil-Based or Shellac Primer

Photo by [Point3D Commercial Imaging Ltd.](https://unsplash.com/@3dottawa?utm_source=seniorstrength&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/?utm_source=seniorstrength&utm_medium=referral)

This is the most important step in the entire process. A quality oil-based primer seals the wallpaper, prevents moisture from reaching the glue underneath, and creates a solid surface for your paint to bond to.

Begin by cutting in around the edges with your angled brush, working carefully along the ceiling, baseboards, and corners. Then use your roller to cover the main sections of the wall in smooth, overlapping strokes.

Apply the primer in a thin, even coat. You are not trying to cover the color of the wallpaper completely at this stage ,  you are sealing and bonding. Allow the primer to dry fully according to the manufacturer’s instructions, which is usually two to four hours.

Once the primer is dry, inspect the surface again. If any new bubbles or seams have appeared, let the primer cure fully, then re-glue and re-sand those spots before applying a second thin coat of primer.

## Step 4: Sand and Smooth the Surface

After the primer has cured, lightly sand the entire wall with your medium-grit sandpaper. This helps knock down any texture from the wallpaper pattern and creates a smoother base for your paint.

You do not need to press hard or sand aggressively. A light, even pass over the whole surface is all that is needed. Wipe away the dust with a clean, dry cloth or a barely damp sponge.

If you notice the wallpaper pattern is particularly textured, you may want to apply a thin skim coat of joint compound over the entire wall before this sanding step. This takes more time but gives a remarkably smooth finish that hides the original pattern beautifully.

## Step 5: Apply Your Paint

Photo by [Wilhelm Gunkel](https://unsplash.com/@wilhelmgunkel?utm_source=seniorstrength&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/?utm_source=seniorstrength&utm_medium=referral)

Now for the rewarding part. Choose a high-quality interior latex paint in the finish you prefer. Eggshell and satin finishes are great choices for most rooms because they are easy to wipe clean and hide minor imperfections better than flat paint.

Just like with the primer, start by cutting in around the edges with your brush, then roll the main wall areas. Use long, smooth strokes and work in sections from top to bottom. Maintain a wet edge as you go to avoid visible lap marks.

Most walls will need two coats of paint for full, even coverage. Allow the first coat to dry completely before applying the second. Between coats, do a quick visual inspection for any areas that need a touch-up.

Stand back and admire your work as the final coat dries. The transformation can be remarkable, especially if you have chosen a fresh, light color to brighten the room.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will the wallpaper pattern show through the paint?

It can, especially if the wallpaper has a heavy texture or raised pattern. Using the right primer and sanding between coats will minimize this significantly. For very textured wallpaper, applying a skim coat of joint compound before priming will give you the smoothest result.

### Can I use a regular latex primer instead of oil-based?

It is strongly recommended that you do not use a water-based latex primer over wallpaper. The water in the primer can soak through the paper and reactivate the original wallpaper adhesive, causing the paper to bubble, wrinkle, or peel away from the wall. Always use an oil-based or shellac primer for this project.

### What if the wallpaper starts to bubble after I prime it?

Small bubbles may appear as the primer dries. Allow the primer to cure completely ,  sometimes bubbles will shrink back down as they dry. If they remain, slice them carefully with a utility knife, re-glue the edges, press flat, and let dry before lightly sanding and re-priming that spot.
