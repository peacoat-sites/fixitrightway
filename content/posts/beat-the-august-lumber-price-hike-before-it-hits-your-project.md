The coverage you're reading about lumber tariffs mostly focuses on what's coming. Here's what's already happening: framing lumber costs climbed 4.4% in a single month as of July 2026, and suppliers aren't waiting for August to adjust their prices. The 35.9% combined tariff burden on Canadian softwood, which now includes a Section 232 tariff, is already baked into what you'll pay at the yard this week. If you have a deck, fence, addition, or structural repair on your list, the window to buy at anything resembling pre-tariff pricing is closing fast.

This isn't a "prices might go up" story. Framing lumber hit $916.62 per thousand board feet in Q2 2026, a 5.11% jump from the prior quarter and the highest point in nine consecutive quarters of year-over-year growth, according to Gordian's July 2026 data. The RoMac Whole House Commodity Index just hit $55,287 in July, the highest level of the year, with Southern Pine 2x12s up 10.7% and 2x6s up 7.5% in a single month. One month.

Average [DIY project](/diy-project-tool-costs-timeline-2026/) spend hit $5,368 in Q1 2026, up roughly 35% from Q1 2025, per the Farnsworth Group. That number is going higher.

## What the Tariff Actually Does to Your Material List

The 35.9% figure sounds abstract until you start pricing a project. Canada supplies the majority of the softwood lumber used in U.S. residential construction, and that tariff doesn't land evenly across every product. Dimensional framing lumber, the 2x4s, 2x6s, 2x8s, 2x10s, and 2x12s you use for walls, floors, and decks, is where the hit is sharpest.

Here's where prices stood heading into August, based on July 2026 reporting:

| Lumber Product | July 2026 Monthly Change | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Southern Pine 2x12 | +10.7% | Structural, deck framing, headers |
| Southern Pine 2x6 | +7.5% | Wall framing, deck joists |
| Framing lumber (general) | +4.4% month-over-month | Broad category, Q2 avg $916.62/MBF |
| Imported Ipe / Cumaru | +15–25% year-over-year | Deck hardwoods, already priced in |
| Overall commodity index | +1.8% monthly | RoMac index at $55,287 in July |

The hardwood story is its own problem. Ipe and Cumaru aren't Canadian softwood, but tariff pressure across the global supply chain has pushed those exotic [deck boards](/should-you-replace-deck-boards-with-wood-or-composite-this-summer/) 15–25% above what you'd have paid in early 2025, according to LDN Decks. If you quoted a premium deck last year and haven't pulled the trigger, that quote is wrong.

## Which Projects Feel the Most Pain

Not every job gets hit equally. Rank your exposure like this:

Structural framing additions, garage builds, or anything requiring significant dimensional lumber takes the hardest shot. A modest room addition might use 3,000 to 5,000 board feet of framing material. At the current rate trajectory, waiting two months on a project like that could mean $400 to $700 more in lumber alone before you drive a single nail.

Deck builds are a close second, especially if you're using pressure-treated Southern Pine for the substructure. The 2x8s and 2x10s that make up a typical deck frame are exactly the products seeing the steepest spikes. Composite decking has its own tariff exposure if components are imported, but PT lumber is the live wire [right now](/how-to-shop-for-materials-right-now-and-beat-tariff-price-hikes/).

Fences and outbuildings feel it less dramatically, but you're still buying dimensional lumber and treated posts. The savings from buying now versus September are real, just smaller in absolute dollars.

## How to Buy Smart Right Now

Buying ahead only makes sense if you have a real project and real storage. Don't speculate on lumber you won't use.

If you do have a project: get your cut list done today, price it at three suppliers this week, and ask each one whether they're holding July pricing into August. Some independent yards are. The big boxes almost certainly aren't.

Buying in unit quantities, full bundles rather than piece by piece, often gets you better pricing and ensures consistent grade and moisture content in your framing stock. Ask about mill-direct pricing at local yards; the markup chain shortens considerably.

One honest caveat on species substitution: if your plans spec Southern Pine for structural members and your yard is pushing a substitution, verify span ratings before you accept it. Not all dimensional lumber is interchangeable at the same dimensions, and a well-intentioned swap can create a code issue.

## When to Call a Pro Instead of DIYing Through This

Material costs rising 35% year-over-year changes the DIY math. You used to save meaningfully on labor by doing it yourself, and the materials were the fixed cost. Now materials are the volatile cost, and a pro contractor who buys at volume might actually be buying your lumber cheaper than you can at retail.

Get a contractor bid on any structural project before you assume DIY is cheaper. A framer who buys 50,000 board feet a month is negotiating rates you can't touch at the lumberyard counter. That advantage disappears on finish work, painting, and landscaping, where materials are a smaller fraction of the total, but for framing-heavy jobs it's worth checking.

For decks in the $15,000 to $30,000 range, the contractor's material cost might be 8 to 12% lower than yours even before you count your own labor time. That gap has widened in 2026. Know the number before you decide.

## When the Window Actually Closes

Suppliers are already adjusting. The formal August tariff date gives this a headline, but the price increases started earlier and will continue past it. The most useful framing here isn't "buy before August 1" as a hard deadline. It's that every week you wait in July and August is a week of additional price drift on a commodity that's been climbing for nine straight quarters.

If your project is real, your plans are done, and your permits are in hand or in process, buy your lumber now. If you're still in the planning phase, move faster than you normally would. The cost of hesitating on material timing is not hypothetical this summer.
