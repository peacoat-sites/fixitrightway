Your furnace is banging, rattling, or squealing at 2 a.m., and you're wondering if this means a $1,500 repair bill. The truth is simpler. Most furnace noise has an easy fix, and you can probably handle it yourself without calling anyone.

## Why Is My Furnace Making Noise?

Every sound your furnace makes is a clue. Banging at startup sounds nothing like a constant rattle, and they need different fixes.

Banging or booming usually means delayed ignition or air ducts expanding as they heat up. Rattling is almost always loose panels or screws that have come undone over the years. A squealing noise points straight to the blower belt or dry motor bearings. Rumbling while the burners are firing can mean they're dirty or the pilot light is acting up. And persistent clicking when the furnace won't ignite? That's likely an ignition problem.

Ignore a strange sound and it'll get worse. A small rattle today becomes a mechanical failure in six months.

## Tools and Materials You Will Need

Grab these before you start. You'll spend maybe $20 to $30 total, and most of it you'll use again on future projects.

Screwdrivers (flathead and Phillips), an adjustable wrench, a replacement furnace filter, HVAC duct tape or foil tape, non-detergent motor oil or 3-in-1 oil, a replacement blower belt if needed, a flashlight, a vacuum with a hose, and some zip ties or metal screws for securing panels. Hardware stores stock all of it.

**⚠️ Safety:** Kill the power at the breaker before you touch anything inside the furnace. If you smell gas, leave and call your gas company from outside. Never work on a running unit.

## Step 1: Check and Replace the Air Filter

A clogged filter makes the blower work overtime, and that's when the noise starts. Pull out the filter (usually near the return air duct or inside the cabinet) and hold it up to light. Can't see through it? Replace it now.

Standard 1-inch filters need changing every month or two. The thicker 4-inch versions last about six months. Grab a MERV 8 to 11 rated filter for the best balance between filtration and airflow. Once it's in, turn the furnace back on and listen. You'll hear the difference immediately in most cases.

## Step 2: Tighten Loose Panels and Screws

Years of heating cycles cause panels to shift. Press every panel on the furnace cabinet firmly while listening. If the rattle stops when you apply pressure, you found it.

Tighten any loose screws with a Phillips head. Missing screws? Hardware stores have replacements for a dollar or two. Foam weather stripping between panels and the frame reduces vibration too. While you're at it, seal your ductwork seams with HVAC foil tape. It quiets the noise and saves energy by plugging air leaks.

## Step 3: Lubricate the Blower Motor and Bearings

A high-pitched squeal coming from the blower? The motor bearings are dry. Turn off the furnace and locate the blower compartment, usually behind the lower access panel.

Look for small oil ports (rubber caps) on the motor housing. Put 2 to 3 drops of non-detergent motor oil or 3-in-1 oil in each one. Don't overdo it. Too much oil damages the motor. Some motors have sealed bearings and can't be lubricated, which means replacement is the only option.

Reassemble everything, restore power, and run the furnace for 10 minutes. The squealing should stop.

## Step 4: Inspect and Replace the Blower Belt

Older furnaces have a belt-driven blower that squeals when the belt wears out. Open the blower compartment and check the belt for smoothness and flexibility. Glazed, frayed, or stiff belts need replacement right away.

Find the belt size printed on the old one or check your furnace manual. Slip the new belt onto the motor pulley first, then onto the blower pulley. You want about half an inch of give in the belt. Not tight, not loose. These cost $5 to $15 and take under 30 minutes to swap out.

## Step 5: Address Banging Ducts and Delayed Ignition

Booming at startup and banging ductwork are two of the loudest problems, but both are fixable.

### Fixing Banging Ductwork

Metal ducts expand and contract, and thin sections can rattle like crazy. Some people call this "oil canning." Add bracing or sheet metal screws to stiffen flexible sections. Check that all vents are open throughout the house. If the problem's bad enough, upgrade to thicker, higher-quality duct material.

### Fixing Delayed Ignition

A loud boom when the furnace fires up means gas is accumulating before igniting. Dirty burners or a blocked igniter cause this. Turn off gas and power, then use a vacuum to gently remove dust from the burner ports. If the boom persists, call an HVAC tech. Delayed ignition damages the heat exchanger over time if you leave it alone.

## When to Call a Professional

Some problems are beyond DIY territory. Stop and call someone if you smell gas near the furnace, hear loud screeching from inside the heat exchanger, get persistent clicking that won't stop, your carbon monoxide detector goes off, or your furnace is older than 20 years and making multiple types of noise.

An HVAC pro can do a full tune-up to prevent future noise and boost efficiency.
