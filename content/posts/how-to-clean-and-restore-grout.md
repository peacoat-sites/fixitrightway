Your tile floors looked beautiful when they were first installed, but now the grout lines have turned dark, dingy, and even a little embarrassing. Dirty grout is one of the most common household frustrations, and it can make an entire room look neglected even when everything else is spotless. The good news is that learning **how to clean grout** is easier than you might think, and you don’t need to hire an expensive professional to get the job done right.

Whether you’re dealing with bathroom tiles, kitchen floors, or an entryway that sees heavy foot traffic, this step-by-step guide will walk you through everything you need to restore your grout to its former glory. Even if you’re not particularly handy, you can absolutely do this yourself and feel proud of the results.

## Why Grout Gets So Dirty

Grout is a porous material, which means it absorbs moisture, soap residue, dirt, and bacteria over time. Because grout lines sit slightly lower than tile surfaces, they act like little trenches that trap everything that passes over them.

In bathrooms, mold and mildew are the biggest culprits. In kitchens and entryways, food particles, grease, and tracked-in soil are the main offenders. Once grout becomes stained, regular mopping alone simply won’t restore it.

## Tools and Materials You Will Need

Before you begin, gather everything you need so you’re not making multiple trips to the store. Having the right supplies on hand makes the job go much more smoothly.

  * Stiff-bristled grout brush or old toothbrush
  * Baking soda
  * White distilled vinegar (avoid on natural stone tiles)
  * Hydrogen peroxide (3% solution from the drugstore)
  * Dish soap or a dedicated grout cleaner
  * Spray bottle
  * Warm water and a bucket
  * Clean microfiber cloths or rags
  * Rubber gloves
  * Safety glasses (optional but recommended)
  * Grout sealer (for the finishing step)
  * Kneeling pad or foam mat for comfort

Most of these items are already in your home or are inexpensive to pick up at any hardware or grocery store. A kneeling pad is especially worth the small investment,  your knees will thank you later!

## Step 1: Sweep and Prepare the Area

Start by thoroughly sweeping or vacuuming the entire tiled area. You want to remove all loose dirt, dust, and debris before introducing any moisture or cleaning solutions.

Next, fill your bucket with warm water and do a basic mop or wipe-down of the tiles. This softens surface grime and prepares the grout lines for deeper cleaning. Let the floor dry for about five minutes before moving on.

Good preparation makes every step that follows more effective. Don’t skip this part,  cleaning over dry, loose debris just pushes the dirt around rather than removing it.

**Pro Tip:** Work in small sections of about two to three square feet at a time. This keeps your cleaning solution from drying out before you scrub it, and it makes the job feel much more manageable rather than overwhelming.

## Step 2: Apply Your Cleaning Solution

For most grout, a simple paste made from baking soda and water is your best starting point. Mix two parts baking soda with one part warm water to form a thick paste, then apply it directly onto the grout lines using your brush or an old toothbrush.

For tougher stains, upgrade to a mixture of baking soda and hydrogen peroxide. Combine half a cup of baking soda with a quarter cup of hydrogen peroxide and a teaspoon of dish soap. This combination is highly effective at lifting deep-set stains without being too harsh.

If you have mold or mildew, spray undiluted white vinegar onto the grout first and let it sit for five minutes before applying the baking soda paste. The fizzing reaction helps break up mold at its roots.

**⚠️ Safety:** Never mix bleach with vinegar, ammonia, or hydrogen peroxide. Combining these chemicals can create toxic fumes that are dangerous to breathe, especially in an enclosed bathroom. Always work in a well-ventilated space, and wear rubber gloves to protect your skin from any cleaning agents.

## Step 3: Scrub the Grout Lines

Photo by [Kamil Klyta](https://unsplash.com/@kamilklyta?utm_source=seniorstrength&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/?utm_source=seniorstrength&utm_medium=referral)

This is the most important step in the entire process, and it does require a little elbow grease. Using your stiff-bristled grout brush, scrub along each grout line in a back-and-forth motion with firm, steady pressure.

A dedicated grout brush with a narrow bristle head works best because it fits directly into the grout channel. An old toothbrush works well for tight corners and edges around fixtures or along baseboards.

Let the cleaning paste sit on the grout for at least five to ten minutes before scrubbing if the staining is severe. This gives the active ingredients time to penetrate and loosen the grime before you start working.

  * Use short, firm strokes rather than long sweeping ones
  * Apply slightly more pressure on dark or heavily stained areas
  * Re-apply paste if it dries out before you finish scrubbing
  * Work from the back of the room toward the door so you don’t kneel on cleaned areas

## Step 4: Rinse Thoroughly

Once you’ve scrubbed all the grout lines in a section, rinse the area well with clean warm water. Use a clean cloth or mop to wipe away all the cleaning residue and loosened dirt.

Leftover cleaning solution, especially anything containing soap, can attract new dirt if it’s not fully removed. Rinse twice if needed to make sure all residue is gone.

Stand back and evaluate your work as you go. You’ll likely be amazed at the difference even after cleaning just a small section. If some stubborn spots remain, repeat the application and scrubbing process before moving on.

## Step 5: Dry and Assess the Results

Photo by [Spencer Plouzek](https://unsplash.com/@theguccer2100?utm_source=seniorstrength&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/?utm_source=seniorstrength&utm_medium=referral)

Allow the cleaned area to dry completely before walking on it or applying any sealer. Open windows or turn on a bathroom exhaust fan to speed up drying time and help prevent mold from returning.

Once dry, take a good look at your grout in bright light. In most cases, cleaning will restore grout to a noticeably lighter, cleaner color. However, if certain areas still look gray or stained despite thorough cleaning, you may be looking at permanent discoloration that requires grout paint or re-grouting rather than just cleaning.

For very old or deeply discolored grout, a grout colorant or grout paint pen can be a wonderful solution. These products are available at hardware stores and are surprisingly easy to apply.

## Step 6: Seal the Grout to Protect It

Sealing your grout after cleaning is one of the most important things you can do to keep it looking clean longer. Grout sealer creates an invisible protective barrier that prevents moisture, stains, and mold from penetrating the porous surface.

Apply sealer using a small brush or the applicator tip on the bottle, running it carefully along each grout line. Wipe away any excess sealer from the tile surface immediately with a clean cloth before it dries.

  * Allow grout to dry completely (at least 24 hours) before sealing
  * Choose a penetrating sealer for the best long-term protection
  * Re-seal every one to two years, or whenever water stops beading on the surface
  * Avoid sealing colored or epoxy grout without checking the manufacturer’s instructions first

Sealing is a step many people skip, but it makes a dramatic difference in how easy your grout is to keep clean going forward. It’s worth the extra thirty minutes of effort.

## Keeping Your Grout Clean Going Forward

Once your grout is clean and sealed, simple maintenance habits will keep it looking great between deep cleanings. A little prevention goes a long way.

  * Wipe up spills immediately before they can soak into the grout
  * Spray a light mist of vinegar solution on bathroom grout weekly to prevent mold buildup
  * Use a squeegee on shower walls after each use to minimize moisture
  * Mop tiled floors regularly with a pH-neutral floor cleaner
  * Do a light grout scrub every few months to stay ahead of buildup


## You’ve Got This!

Cleaning and restoring grout is one of those home tasks that delivers a truly satisfying result. With the right tools, a little patience, and the simple steps outlined above, you can transform tired, dingy tile floors and walls into something you’re genuinely proud of.

Take your time, work in sections, and don’t forget to seal when you’re done. Your floors have a long, clean life ahead of them,  and so do you. Happy cleaning!
