Your tile floors looked beautiful [when they were first installed](/how-to-level-an-uneven-floor-before-tiling/), but now the grout lines have turned dark, dingy, and even a little embarrassing. Dirty grout is one of the most common household frustrations, and it can make an entire room look neglected even when everything else is spotless. The good news is that learning **how to clean grout** is easier than you might think, and you don't need to hire an expensive professional to get the job done right.

Whether you're dealing with bathroom tiles, kitchen floors, or an entryway that sees heavy foot traffic, this step-by-step guide will walk you through everything you need to restore your grout to its former glory. Even if you're not particularly handy, you can absolutely do this yourself and feel proud of the results.

## Why Grout Gets So Dirty

Grout is porous. That's the real problem. It absorbs moisture, soap residue, dirt, and bacteria like a sponge, and because grout lines sit slightly lower than tile surfaces, they trap everything that passes over them.

In bathrooms, mold and mildew are the biggest culprits. In kitchens and entryways, food particles, grease, and tracked-in soil pile up. Once grout becomes stained, regular mopping alone won't cut it. You need to actually clean it.

## Tools and Materials You Will Need

Gather everything before you start so you're not running back to the store mid-project. Having supplies on hand makes this go fast.

  * Stiff-bristled grout brush or old toothbrush
  * Baking soda
  * White distilled vinegar (avoid on natural stone tiles)
  * Hydrogen peroxide (3% solution from the drugstore)
  * Dish soap or a dedicated grout cleaner
  * Spray bottle
  * Warm water and a bucket
  * Clean microfiber cloths or rags
  * Rubber gloves
  * Safety glasses (optional but recommended)
  * Grout sealer (for the finishing step)
  * Kneeling pad or foam mat for comfort

Most of this stuff is already at home or costs just a few dollars at any hardware or grocery store. A kneeling pad is worth the investment, your knees will thank you later.

## Step 1: Sweep and Prepare the Area

Photo by [JUNHYUNG PARK](https://unsplash.com/@nangmanist?utm_source=seniorstrength&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/?utm_source=seniorstrength&utm_medium=referral)

Thoroughly sweep or vacuum the entire tiled area first. You want all loose dirt, dust, and debris gone before any moisture hits.

Fill your bucket with warm water and do a basic mop or wipe-down of the tiles. This softens surface grime and preps the grout for deeper cleaning. Let it dry for about five minutes.

Good prep makes everything that follows work better. Don't skip this, cleaning over dry debris just pushes dirt around instead of removing it.

**Pro Tip:** Work in small sections of about two to three square feet at a time. This prevents your cleaning solution from drying out before you scrub, and it feels way less overwhelming.

## Step 2: Apply Your Cleaning Solution

For most grout, start with baking soda and water. Mix three parts baking soda with one part warm water until you get a thick paste, then apply it directly onto the grout lines.

For tougher stains, use baking soda with hydrogen peroxide instead. Combine half a cup of baking soda with a quarter cup of hydrogen peroxide and a teaspoon of dish soap. This hits deep-set stains hard without being too harsh.

If you've got mold or mildew, spray undiluted white vinegar first and let it sit for five minutes before applying paste. The fizzing reaction breaks up mold at its source.

**⚠️ Safety:** Never mix bleach with vinegar, ammonia, or hydrogen peroxide. This creates toxic fumes that'll make you seriously sick, especially in a closed bathroom. Always work somewhere with fresh air, and wear gloves to protect your skin.

## Step 3: Scrub the Grout Lines

Photo by [Kamil Klyta](https://unsplash.com/@kamilklyta?utm_source=seniorstrength&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/?utm_source=seniorstrength&utm_medium=referral)

This is where the work happens. Using your stiff-bristled brush, scrub along each grout line with firm, back-and-forth motions.

A dedicated grout brush with a narrow bristle head works best because it fits directly into the grout channel. An old toothbrush handles tight corners and edges around fixtures.

If staining is bad, let the paste sit for five to ten minutes before scrubbing. This gives the ingredients time to penetrate and loosen grime.

  * Use short, firm strokes rather than long sweeping ones
  * Apply slightly more pressure on dark or heavily stained areas
  * Re-apply paste if it dries out before you finish scrubbing
  * Work from the back of the room toward the door so you don't kneel on cleaned areas

## Step 4: Rinse Thoroughly

Once you've scrubbed a section, rinse it well with clean warm water. Wipe away all the cleaning residue and loosened dirt with a clean cloth or mop.

Leftover soap can attract new dirt if it's not fully removed. Rinse twice if needed.

Stand back and look at your progress. You'll likely be amazed at even one small section. If stubborn spots remain, repeat the application and scrubbing before moving on.

## Step 5: Dry and Assess the Results

Photo by [Spencer Plouzek](https://unsplash.com/@theguccer2100?utm_source=seniorstrength&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/?utm_source=seniorstrength&utm_medium=referral)

Let the cleaned area dry completely before walking on it or sealing it. Open windows or turn on a bathroom exhaust fan to speed drying and prevent mold from creeping back.

Once dry, look at your grout in bright light. In most cases, cleaning restores grout to a noticeably lighter color. But if areas still look gray or stained after thorough cleaning, you're probably looking at permanent discoloration. Grout paint or re-grouting is your solution then.

For very old or deeply discolored grout, a grout colorant or grout paint pen works great. These are easy to apply and available at any hardware store.

## Step 6: Seal the Grout to Protect It

Sealing grout after cleaning might be the single most important thing you do to keep it clean longer. Sealer creates an invisible barrier that stops moisture, stains, and mold from sinking in.

Apply sealer with a small brush or the applicator tip on the bottle, running it along each grout line. Wipe away excess sealer from tile immediately with a clean cloth before it dries.

  * Let grout dry completely (at least 24 hours) before sealing
  * Use a penetrating sealer for best long-term protection
  * Re-seal every one to two years, or when water stops beading on the surface
  * Check the manufacturer's instructions for colored or epoxy grout before sealing

Most people skip this step, but it makes a huge difference in how easy grout stays to clean. It's worth thirty minutes of effort.

## Keeping Your Grout Clean Going Forward

Once your grout is clean and sealed, simple habits keep it looking great between deep cleanings. Prevention works.

  * Wipe up spills immediately before they soak into grout
  * Spray bathroom grout with a light vinegar solution weekly to prevent mold
  * Use a squeegee on shower walls after each use to minimize moisture
  * Mop tiled floors regularly with a pH-neutral floor cleaner
  * Do a light grout scrub every few months to stay ahead of buildup
