You pull the grill cover off for the first time this season, grab the wire brush hanging on the side, and start scrubbing the grates without a second thought. That's exactly what 13 million other households were doing right up until federal regulators stepped in and said: stop.

Weber recalled 3.2 million metal wire-bristle grill brushes on February 26, 2026. A month later, on March 26, Nexgrill followed with a recall of 10.2 million brushes, sold exclusively through Home Depot and its website between 2015 and 2026 for anywhere from $5 to $15. Together, these two actions represent one of the largest grilling-product recalls in CPSC history, and they landed right at the start of grilling season, when most people are least likely to be checking recall databases.

If you've got a wire brush in your garage or hanging by the grill, there's a very real chance it's one of them.

## What Actually Went Wrong (And Why It's More Serious Than It Sounds)

The hazard isn't the brush breaking or the handle cracking. It's the bristles. Tiny metal wires detach from the brush head, land on the grill grate, stick to food, and get swallowed. You don't necessarily feel it going down. The problem shows up later, sometimes in an emergency room.

The CPSC received at least 68 reports of bristles detaching from Nexgrill brushes alone. Five of those cases required medical treatment to remove bristles from someone's digestive tract or throat. That's not a rounding error. A wire bristle lodged in soft tissue is a genuine medical emergency, and the insidious part is that neither the cook nor anyone eating the food has any idea it happened until something goes wrong hours later.

I've been around enough job sites to know that "rare but serious" is exactly the risk category worth paying attention to. The everyday probability is low. The consequence when it happens is not.

## How to Find Out If Your Brush Is Recalled

For the Nexgrill recall, check any wire grill brush you bought from Home Depot since 2015. The recall covers brushes sold under the Nexgrill brand in that window, and given how many were sold, the odds are reasonable that something in your garage qualifies. The CPSC recall page has the specific model details if you want to cross-reference.

Weber's recall covers their wire-bristle brushes sold through retailers nationwide. Weber is handling their recall differently from Nexgrill: they're offering a free replacement brush, specifically a nylon-bristle model, rather than a refund. Nexgrill, through Home Depot, is issuing a full gift-card refund of $5 to $15 depending on the model. Neither remedy requires you to ship anything back. You stop using the brush, and then you contact the company.

The actual steps: for Nexgrill, start at the CPSC recall page or Home Depot's recall portal. For Weber, go directly to Weber's website or the CPSC recall page filed February 26, 2026. Both companies want you to dispose of the brush, not return it in person.

Don't procrastinate on this. Recalls have participation windows, and these are already several months old.

## What to Use Instead

Here's where I want to give you something actually useful, because the recall response from most sources is "throw it away" without telling you what comes next.

Safety experts and emergency physicians are now recommending people step away from the wire-bristle category entirely, not just the recalled models. The bristle-detachment problem isn't unique to Nexgrill and Weber. It's a design issue inherent to how these brushes are built. The wires loosen with heat cycling and repeated use, and there's no obvious way to tell when a brush has crossed into dangerous territory.

The alternatives that work well in practice:

**Grill stones and pumice blocks** are exactly what they sound like, a dense abrasive block you drag across a hot grate. They wear down over time and leave residue on the grate, so you want to do a quick burn-off after using one, but they're effective and there's nothing to detach.

**Coiled stainless steel scrapers** use a spiral of heavy-gauge wire rather than fine bristles. The coils are too large to end up embedded in food, and they're surprisingly aggressive on carbon buildup. Brands like Grill Rescue have made this format mainstream. Prices run $20 to $35, which is more than a $10 Nexgrill brush, but you're buying it once.

**Wooden scrapers** work best when you use them on a hot grate immediately after cooking, letting the grate's own surface shape the wood's edge over time. They're less effective on really built-up carbon but fine for regular maintenance.

I've used coiled scrapers for the last two seasons and I don't miss the wire brush. The learning curve is about thirty seconds.

## Don't Just Toss It, Claim Your Remedy First

What most people don't realize is that you're entitled to something here, and leaving money (or a free brush) on the table because you didn't fill out a form is a frustrating outcome for a two-minute task.

If you have a Nexgrill brush, you're looking at a $5 to $15 gift card. That's not life-changing, but it's real money toward a replacement. Weber's free nylon brush replacement is actually a decent deal given what a nylon brush costs at retail. According to coverage from Bob Vila and Today.com, both companies are processing remedies without requiring you to mail anything in, which removes the main friction point that usually kills recall participation rates.

Document what you have before you toss it. Take a photo of the brush, the label if it still has one, and note where you bought it and roughly when. That's all the information you'll typically need to submit a claim.

One more thing: if you or someone in your household experienced any symptoms after grilling this season and you're now connecting dots, the CPSC wants those reports. The 68 reports that triggered the Nexgrill recall came from real people filing complaints, and that data is what forces action. You can file at SaferProducts.gov regardless of whether you need medical attention.

The timing here is genuinely terrible for consumers. Grilling season in full swing, and two of the most common grill tools on the market yanked off shelves at once. But the replacement options are real, the remedies are straightforward, and you can be grill-ready with a safer tool by this weekend.
