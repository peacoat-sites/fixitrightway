If you've got a wire grill brush sitting in your garage, you might be holding onto a recalled one. You're definitely not alone. Two massive recalls hit in the first three months of 2026, both timed perfectly to wreck your summer grilling plans: Weber recalled 3.2 million brushes on February 26, and Nexgrill followed up on March 26 with 10.2 million more. That's 13.4 million brushes that shouldn't be anywhere near your food.

## How to Know If Your Brush Is Recalled

| Brand | Recall Date | Units Recalled | Sold At | Price Range | Model Numbers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nexgrill | March 26, 2026 | 10.2 million | Home Depot | $5-$15 | 530-0024, 530-0024G, 530-0034, 530-0039, 530-0041, 530-0042 |
| Weber | February 26, 2026 | 3.2 million | Lowe's, Home Depot, Ace Hardware, Target, Amazon | Not specified | Not specified |

Start with the brand. For Nexgrill, the recall covers brushes sold at Home Depot (in-store and online) between 2015 and 2026, priced $5 to $15. Check your brush handle for one of these model numbers: 530-0024, 530-0024G, 530-0034, 530-0039, 530-0041, or 530-0042. [The CPSC's March 26 recall notice](https://cpsc.gov/Recalls/2026/Nexgrill-Recalls-Over-10-2-Million-Metal-Wire-Bristle-Grill-Brushes-Due-to-Ingestion-Hazard-Sold-at-Home-Depot) has the full details. If you can't find the packaging, check your Home Depot purchase history or call Nexgrill directly.

Weber's broader. Those brushes showed up at Lowe's, Home Depot, Ace Hardware, Target, and Amazon from 2011 through 2026. If you've owned a Weber wire brush for more than a few years, assume it's affected and verify through Weber's claims portal.

These brushes look totally harmless. That's exactly why they're dangerous.

## Why This Hazard Is So Serious

Don't dismiss this as regulatory overreach. The CPSC logged 68 reports of Nexgrill bristles coming loose, including five people who needed emergency treatment to get them removed from their throats or digestive systems. A wire bristle is impossibly thin. It sticks to your grate, transfers to food invisibly, gets swallowed without anyone noticing, and then perforates something it shouldn't.

CPSC Chair Peter Feldman called it "a dangerous design defect that allows metal bristles to detach and be swallowed without detection" in his [February 26 statement on the Weber recall](https://www.cpsc.gov/About-CPSC/Chairman/Peter-A-Feldman/Statement/Chairman-Feldman-Statement-on-Weber-Metal-Wire-Bristle-Grill-Brush-Recall-Manufacturers-Must-Address-Known-Hazards-in-Metal-Wire-Bristle-Grill-Brushes). That's unusually blunt language from a regulator. They're not hedging.

Here's what makes it worse: you won't taste it. You won't feel it. Your kid definitely won't inspect a hot dog before taking a bite.

## How to Get Your Refund

Do this now. Both companies made the process quick, and it takes five minutes.

Nexgrill claims go through nexgrill.mktpoint.com/recall. You'll get a gift card for whatever you paid ($5 to $15 depending on the model). It's not going to buy you dinner, but it's your money back for something you can't use anymore. Weber has a separate claims process on their website. Either way, you'll need to destroy the brush and probably send a photo to prove it.

Don't donate it. Don't give it to a neighbor. Destroy it now, then file your claim on your phone.

## What to Actually Use Instead

Most articles gloss over this part. Let me give you actual options.

**Bristle-free spiral brushes** use coiled stainless steel instead of wire bristles, so nothing sheds. Grillart and Kona both make solid versions in the $15 to $30 range. They clean standard grates well and last a full season.

**Grill stones** (sometimes called pumice blocks) drag across a warm grate to scrub off carbon and grease with zero shedding risk. They wear down eventually but cost $8 to $12 to replace.

**Wooden scrapers** work if your grate has a consistent pattern. Press the scraper against hot metal a few times and it grooves to match your specific grate. Not as aggressive as a coil brush, but they handle regular post-cook maintenance fine.

[HGTV's March 31 coverage](https://www.hgtv.com/gardening/gardening-tips/metal-wire-bristle-grill-brush-recall) notes that the CPSC recommends inspecting your grates even after you switch tools. I'd go further: check the grate before you put food on it, not just after you clean it. Two seconds, literally.

## The Bigger Picture

These recalls didn't come out of nowhere. Safety researchers have flagged wire bristle brushes for years. What changed in early 2026 is the regulatory pressure finally kicked in, the scale exploded, and the timing landed directly on you before Independence Day weekend.

Wondering if you're overreacting? You're not. A $10 to $25 replacement brush is one of the lowest-stakes safety calls you'll make this year. The risk of doing nothing isn't worth it.

Find your model number, claim your refund, and grab a coil brush or stone before your next cookout. That's the whole thing.
