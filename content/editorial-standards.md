# How Fix It Right Way Is Written

Fix It Right Way explains home repairs and maintenance for homeowners: what a job involves, what it costs, and when it makes sense to do it yourself. This page explains, plainly, how the articles are made.

## How articles are made

Articles on Fix It Right Way are drafted with AI writing tools. Each draft starts from public reference sources on the topic, including those listed below, and from recent news when a topic is changing.

Before an article is published, a second automated pass reviews it and removes or rewrites anything it cannot stand behind: statistics, prices or dates it is not confident are correct, studies or organizations that may be misattributed, and personal stories presented as if they happened to the writer.

Articles are not reviewed by a licensed contractor, electrician or plumber before publication. Use them as background for a conversation with one, not as a substitute.

The site owner audits published articles in batches, merging pages that overlap, deleting weak ones and correcting errors.

## Who writes here

The bylines on Fix It Right Way (Dana Morrison, Kim Reeves, Maria Gonzalez, Paul Zhang, Steve Adams) are pen names, one for each area of coverage. They are not real individuals, they hold no professional licenses, and their profile images are illustrations, not photographs of real people.

## Sources we rely on

- [Consumer Product Safety Commission](https://www.cpsc.gov/): Product and home safety
- [ENERGY STAR](https://www.energystar.gov/): Appliance and efficiency guidance
- [U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/): Home energy and repair
- [Penn State Extension](https://extension.psu.edu/): Practical home and property

Where an article depends on a specific figure, rule or study, it links to or names the source so you can check it.

## Limits of this site

Building codes and permit rules vary by city and county, so check with your local building department before electrical, gas, structural or major plumbing work. Switch off power at the breaker and confirm it is off with a tester before touching wiring, and do not work on gas lines yourself. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.

## Corrections

If you find an error, please report it through the [contact page](/contact/). Reports go directly to the site owner, and confirmed errors are corrected in the article.

## How the site makes money

Fix It Right Way may earn money from display advertising and from affiliate links, including Amazon links (as an Amazon Associate, the site earns from qualifying purchases). Advertisers and affiliate partners do not see or approve articles before they are published.
