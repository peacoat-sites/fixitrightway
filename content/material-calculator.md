Buying the right amount of material is one of the most practical skills a DIYer can develop. Order too little and you're making a second trip to the hardware store mid-project — sometimes impossible to match the same dye lot for flooring or tile. Order too much and you're storing leftover materials in the garage for years. Getting the quantity right from the start saves time, money, and frustration.

This multi-mode calculator covers the six most common DIY material estimating tasks: **paint, flooring and tile, concrete, drywall, mulch, and fencing.** Each mode uses the industry-standard formulas that contractors rely on, while accounting for the waste factors that beginners often forget.

**Paint** calculations subtract door and window areas from your total wall square footage, then divide by the standard coverage rate of 350 square feet per gallon. Textured surfaces like orange peel or knockdown require about 15% more paint. **Flooring and tile** calculations add a waste percentage — 5% for straight lay patterns, 10% for offset or diagonal, and up to 15% for herringbone — so your cuts don't leave you short. **Concrete** conversions from linear dimensions to cubic yards are famously confusing; this tool handles the math and shows you both 60 lb and 80 lb bag equivalents so you can compare at the store. **Drywall** estimating counts your wall area, subtracts openings, then adds 10% waste for cuts before dividing by 32 square feet per 4×8 sheet. **Mulch and landscaping** calculations convert your bed dimensions and desired depth to cubic yards and bag equivalents. **Fence** estimating counts posts (including the final end post), horizontal rails, and pickets.

Select the project type from the dropdown at the top of the calculator, fill in your measurements, and the results update instantly.

When you head to the store with your numbers, a few buying tips will save you headaches. For **paint**, always buy in one batch — different production runs can have slight color variation. A single quart for touch-ups is worth adding to your cart. For **flooring**, stick with the same batch number printed on the box end; if you're doing a large room, grab an extra box from the same pallet. For **concrete**, small overages are fine but large overages mean wasted product — mix to the bag's directions, not by eye. For **drywall**, the 10% waste factor already in the calculator handles normal cut-waste; for rooms with many angles or archways, bump it to 15%. For **mulch**, bulk delivery by the yard is almost always cheaper than bagged once you're over about 3 cubic yards. For **fencing**, keep a few extra pickets on hand; boards split, warp, and get damaged during installation more often than you'd expect.

Double-check your measurements before finalizing your order — measure twice, as the old saying goes, because returns on cut or opened materials are rarely accepted. If you're tackling a complex room shape, break it into rectangles and add the results.
